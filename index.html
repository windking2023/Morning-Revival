The Ministry of Reconciliation

1 · TODAY’S SCRIPTURE

2 Corinthians 5:18–20

OUR ENGLISH BIBLE · EXISTING DIRECT TRANSLATION
2 Cor. 5:18And all things are of the God who has reconciled us to Himself by Jesus Christ, and given to us the ministry of that reconciliation:
2 Cor. 5:19How that God was in Christ, reconciling the world to Himself, not reckoning to them their offences; and putting in us the word of that reconciliation.
2 Cor. 5:20We are ambassadors therefore for Christ, God as it were beseeching by us; we entreat for Christ: Be reconciled to God.

Morning Nourishment

In 2 Corinthians 5:18–20 Paul speaks of the ministry of reconciliation. According to verse 20, the ambassadors of Christ are one with God; they are like God, and they entreat as God. Their word is God’s word, and what they do is God’s doing. Furthermore, the words “on behalf of Christ” mean “representing Christ.” The apostles were authorized by Christ to represent Him to do the work of reconciliation.

Paul’s composition in 5:20 is unusual. After saying, “We are ambassadors,” he says, “As God entreats you through us.” Paul seems to be saying, “We are ambassadors of Christ, and we are doing a reconciling work. This is like God entreating you through us. We are one with Christ and one with God. Christ is one with us, and God also is one with us. Therefore, God, Christ, and we, the apostles, are all one.” The ministry of the new covenant is a ministry in which God, Christ, and the ministers are one. God, Christ, and the apostles were one in carrying out the ministry of reconciliation.

(Life-study of 2 Corinthians, pp. 317–318)

2 · 3-MINUTE KEY SUPPLY

From reconciliation received to reconciliation lived

The ministry of reconciliation begins with God, not with us. God reconciles us to Himself through Christ, and then He places the word and ministry of reconciliation in those whom He has reconciled. This means that a Christian minister is not first a person presenting his own ideas. He is an ambassador — a person sent to represent another. The weight of the ministry comes from representing Christ rather than representing the self.

Paul’s appeal, “Be reconciled to God,” is addressed in this context to believers. The Corinthians were already saints and had already been brought to God, yet the ministry still called them to be reconciled further. The supplied Day 1 reading develops this as a second step: a believer may be forgiven and genuinely belong to God, yet still live much of the daily life in the natural man, in the flesh, and from the self.

The first step of reconciliation deals with our sins and brings the sinner back to God. The further step deals with the person who still lives from the natural life. Christ not only died for our sins; He died for us, so that those who live would no longer live to themselves but to Him in resurrection life. Reconciliation therefore is not merely a past event. It becomes a present, practical question: from whom am I living right now — from Christ, or from my natural self?

The outline portrays these two stages by the two veils of the tabernacle. Passing the first veil corresponds to being brought from sin into a relationship with God. Passing the second veil points to being brought further into God’s immediate presence, no longer remaining separated by the natural man. The goal is not simply to know that we are in Christ positionally, but to live in Christ practically and to remain with God in our spirit.

This also determines whether we can minister reconciliation to others. An ambassador can genuinely represent Christ only to the extent that he himself is being reconciled from self, natural preference, and fleshly living into Christ. Day 1 therefore turns the doctrine into a daily exercise: allow the cross to deal with what comes from the natural self, live to Christ in resurrection, and let God gain more room in us. Then the word of reconciliation can pass through us not as a slogan, but as something we are living.

3 · IMPORTANT SENTENCES
Reconciliation begins with forgiveness, but it does not end there; God wants to bring us fully into Himself.
An ambassador of Christ represents the One who sent him, not his own opinion or natural life.
Further reconciliation is the daily crossing from the natural self into a practical living in Christ.

Morning Revival — Week 2 · Day 2
1 · TODAY’S SCRIPTURE

Galatians 2:20; 5:24

OUR ENGLISH BIBLE · EXISTING DIRECT TRANSLATION
Gal. 2:20It is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me. The life I now live in the flesh, I live in faith in the Son of God, who loved me and gave himself for me.
Gal. 5:24Those who belong to Christ Jesus have crucified the flesh with its passions and desires.

Morning Nourishment

Although the Corinthians had been saved and reconciled to God halfway, they still lived in the flesh; that is, they lived in the soul, the outer man, the natural being. The veil of the flesh, of the natural man, still separated them from God. Their natural being was a separating veil; therefore, they needed the second step of reconciliation.

Paul was working on the Corinthians to cut the veil of the flesh asunder, to crucify their natural life, and to consume their outer man so that the believers could enter into the Holy of Holies.

(Life-study of 2 Corinthians, p. 320)

2 · 3-MINUTE KEY SUPPLY

From the natural life into God Himself

Day 2 makes the second step of reconciliation very practical. A believer may truly be saved and yet continue to live much of the daily life from the flesh, the soul, or the natural person. Galatians 2:20 and 5:24 show the way forward: the old “I” has been crucified with Christ, and those who belong to Christ have crucified the flesh with its passions and desires. The issue is not merely improved behavior; it is a change in the source from which we live.

The picture of the Holy Place and the Holy of Holies helps explain this. In the Holy Place there are genuine spiritual blessings, but the reading stresses that God’s direct presence is in the Holy of Holies. Further reconciliation therefore brings us not only out of sin but also out of the natural man, so that we may live before God in our spirit.

This further reconciliation also changes the way we serve. The apostles did not merely work for God; they worked with God. Working for the Lord can still leave us acting independently, according to our own ability or preference. Working with God means that we ourselves are in Him while we serve. Only a person who is in God can bring others into God.

For this reason, our closeness to God becomes the measure of what our work can produce. If we are far from Him in practical living, we cannot bring others very far into Him. The more we are in Him, the more our speaking and service can carry others into the same place.

The day closes by connecting reconciliation with full salvation. In 2 Corinthians 6:2, salvation is not merely the initial salvation of a sinner; in this context it is the present experience of being reconciled to God more fully. The Christian life therefore includes continued salvation from the natural life into a practical living in God.

3 · IMPORTANT SENTENCES
Further reconciliation deals not only with what we do, but with the natural person from whom we live.
To work with God, we must first be in God; our closeness to Him measures how far we can bring others into Him.
Full salvation in this context is the continuing experience of being reconciled to God in every part of our living.

Morning Revival — Week 2 · Day 3
1 · TODAY’S SCRIPTURE

2 Corinthians 5:21; Romans 8:3

OUR ENGLISH BIBLE · EXISTING DIRECT TRANSLATION
2 Cor. 5:21Him who knew not sin he has made sin for us, that we might become God's righteousness in him.
Rom. 8:3For what the law could not do, weakened by the flesh, God did: sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh and for sin, he condemned sin in the flesh,

Morning Nourishment

In 2 Corinthians 5:21 we reach the ultimate consummation of God’s salvation—the righteousness of God. The new covenant ministry is a ministry of the Spirit and of righteousness; it imparts the Spirit of life into the believers and issues in a condition that God can justify.

Yet even after we are saved, matters in our daily living may still be out of harmony with God. The reading points especially to the separation caused by the natural man, the self. Further reconciliation deals with this remaining separation.

(Life-study of 2 Corinthians, p. 321)

2 · 3-MINUTE KEY SUPPLY

From being justified to becoming God’s righteousness

Day 3 deepens the meaning of reconciliation by linking it to the righteousness of God. Second Corinthians 5:21 does not stop with our being forgiven or justified. It says that in Christ we may become God’s righteousness. The emphasis is therefore not merely on a legal standing but on a condition in which our being and living are brought into harmony with God.

The supplied reading uses the example of a husband and wife. Both may be saved and changed outwardly, yet the husband’s strong disposition and the wife’s peculiarity can remain. Their old fighting may stop, but their natural constitution can still keep them from a full experience of God. This is called a halfway reconciliation and a halfway righteousness.

Further reconciliation reaches the disposition, peculiarity, and natural life. When the cross deals with what is natural, the believers can be brought further into the Holy of Holies to enjoy the Lord. Then righteousness is not merely a doctrine; it becomes a living condition in which God is satisfied.

The reading finally uses the image of a butterfly emerging from its cocoon. We may know that we are a new creation in Christ and yet hesitate to say that our living fully expresses God’s righteousness. The remaining “cocoon” represents what of the natural man still needs the cross. As that is dealt with, God gains more expression in us.

The goal points forward to the New Jerusalem, where everything is right, ordered, and satisfactory to God. Day 3 therefore turns righteousness into a present question: in my reactions, relationships, and choices, is there still something natural separating me from God, or is Christ gaining the expression He desires?

3 · IMPORTANT SENTENCES
God’s righteousness is not only that He justifies us; His salvation brings our living into a condition that is right and satisfactory to Him.
Salvation may change our outward conduct while our natural disposition still requires further reconciliation.
The cross works on the remaining “cocoon” of the natural life so that Christ can be expressed as God’s righteousness.

Morning Revival — Week 2 · Day 4
1 · TODAY’S SCRIPTURE

2 Corinthians 5:14; 3:8–9

OUR ENGLISH BIBLE · EXISTING DIRECT TRANSLATION
2 Cor. 5:14For the love of the Christ constrains us, having judged this: that one died for all, then all have died;
2 Cor. 3:8how shall not rather the ministry of the Spirit subsist in glory?
2 Cor. 3:9For if the ministry of condemnation be glory, much rather the ministry of righteousness abounds in glory.

Morning Nourishment

Many Christians know that Christ died for their sins, but Day 4 stresses the further fact that Christ died for us—the persons, the flesh, the old creation. Christ died not only that our sins might be forgiven, but that our outward man and natural being might be terminated so that we may become the righteousness of God.

The new covenant ministry therefore brings us back to God to such an extent that we do not merely stand justified before Him; in Christ we become the righteousness of God.

(Life-study of 2 Corinthians, pp. 323–324)

2 · 3-MINUTE KEY SUPPLY

The Spirit within and righteousness expressed

Day 4 joins two aspects of the new covenant ministry: the Spirit as the inward life supply and righteousness as God’s outward expression. Christ’s death is presented in two directions. He died for our sins so that we might be forgiven; He also died for us so that the natural person could be terminated. The second aspect opens the way for Christ to live in us and be expressed through us.

The ministry of the Spirit is not merely to give us spiritual knowledge. It supplies the life-giving Spirit into our being. As this Spirit lives and works in us, He changes the inward condition from which we act. Righteousness then becomes the spontaneous result of the Spirit’s inward operation.

The reading defines righteousness more deeply than simply “not doing anything wrong.” A person may be outwardly correct while inwardly absent from the Lord’s mind and will. The example of a student who does nothing obviously wrong but does not study properly shows that outward blamelessness can still fall short of being right.

When the life-giving Spirit infuses and saturates us, our inner being becomes transparent and clear. We begin to sense what is in the Lord’s heart and what His will is. Then our relationships, decisions, and even our handling of material things can come under His mind. This is righteousness as God expressed.

Thus the ministry of the new covenant is not merely a ministry that tells people to behave correctly. It ministers Christ as the Spirit until He becomes our inward supply, and it produces a living that is right with God, with others, and with ourselves. That living becomes a visible expression of God.

3 · IMPORTANT SENTENCES
Christ died not only for our sins but also for us, so that the natural person could be terminated and Christ expressed.
Righteousness is deeper than outward correctness; it is a being and living that agree with God’s mind and will.
The Spirit supplies life inwardly, and righteousness is the expression that comes forth outwardly.

1 · TODAY’S SCRIPTURE

Hebrews 9:3; 10:19–20

OUR ENGLISH BIBLE · EXISTING DIRECT TRANSLATION
Heb. 9:3Behind the second curtain was the compartment called the Most Holy Place,
Heb. 10:19Therefore, brothers and sisters, we have confidence to enter the sanctuary through the blood of Jesus,
Heb. 10:20by the new and living way He inaugurated for us through the curtain, that is, His flesh.

Morning Nourishment

The two steps of reconciliation are clearly portrayed by the two veils of the tabernacle. The first veil, the screen, typifies the first step: through the reconciling blood a sinner is brought from the outer court into the Holy Place.

The second veil still separated the Holy Place from the Holy of Holies. It had to be rent so that the believer could be brought further into God’s immediate presence. The Corinthian believers had passed the first veil, but because they still lived in the flesh they needed the second step of reconciliation—to enter the Holy of Holies and live with God in their spirit.

(Life-study of 2 Corinthians, pp. 319–320)

2 · 3-MINUTE KEY SUPPLY

From being brought back to God to being brought into God

Day 5 gives reconciliation a spatial picture. The outer court, the Holy Place, and the Holy of Holies portray different stages of approach to God. The first veil deals with the sinner’s need for forgiveness and brings him into a genuine relationship with God. Yet the second veil shows that a believer can still remain short of God’s immediate presence.

Hebrews 10:19–20 identifies the veil with Christ’s flesh and declares that through His blood a new and living way has been opened into the Holy of Holies. The objective work has been accomplished: the veil has been rent. The practical question is whether we remain in the natural life or pass through in experience to live with God in the spirit.

The day’s reading then expands the meaning of reconciliation. Biblically, to be reconciled is not merely to be brought back near God; it is to be brought into Him. This means an intimate union in which we abide in Him and He abides in us. The reading uses John 15:4 to stress this “in Me and I in you” relationship.

Because of this, the ministry of reconciliation continues among believers. Whenever we live outside of God in our attitudes, choices, or natural reactions, we need to be brought back into Him. Reconciliation is therefore not simply an evangelistic word for unbelievers; it is also a continuing ministry that brings God’s people into a deeper practical oneness with Him.

Day 5 invites a simple exercise: do not settle merely for spiritual blessings in the Holy Place. Through the opened way, turn from the natural self to the spirit, enter God’s presence, and allow the Lord to make His home in the practical details of the day.

3 · IMPORTANT SENTENCES
The first veil brings us from sin to God; the second veil points to being brought from the natural life into God’s immediate presence.
The veil has been rent objectively, but we still need to pass through it in our daily experience.
Reconciliation in its fuller sense is not merely to come back to God, but to be brought into Him and live in mutual abiding.

Morning Revival — Week 2 · Day 6
1 · TODAY’S SCRIPTURE

2 Corinthians 6:11–13

OUR ENGLISH BIBLE · EXISTING DIRECT TRANSLATION
2 Cor. 6:11Our mouth is opened to you, Corinthians, our heart is expanded.
2 Cor. 6:12you are not straitened in us, but you are straitened in your affections;
2 Cor. 6:13but for an answering recompense, (I speak as to children,) let your heart also expand itself.

Morning Nourishment

If we would be fully reconciled to God and fully saved, we need to be enlarged in our heart. The reading recalls strict and straight brothers who were very exact with others but whose narrowness showed that they themselves still needed enlargement.

To be enlarged does not mean to become loose. We should remain strict and straight concerning ourselves, but we should not apply that same strictness to others. An enlarged heart can receive, forgive, and give others room while remaining serious before the Lord.

(Life-study of 2 Corinthians, pp. 361–363)

2 · 3-MINUTE KEY SUPPLY

A large heart as the fruit of fuller reconciliation

Day 6 brings the week’s teaching into our relationships. Paul says, “Our heart is expanded,” and appeals to the Corinthians to be enlarged. The reading connects the size of our heart with the degree of our reconciliation to God. A narrow heart is not merely a personality trait; it can expose areas where we have not yet allowed God to save and reconcile us fully.

An enlarged person is not careless or permissive. The principle is to be strict with ourselves and spacious toward others. When we demand from others the same measure, timing, or manner that we demand from ourselves, we easily become narrow. Further reconciliation frees us from using our own standard as the measure for everyone else.

The reading applies this especially to offenses. In family life and church life, offenses accumulate easily. We may say that we have forgiven, yet continue to keep an inward record. The ministry portion presses the matter further: true forgiving includes forgetting—releasing the offense so that it no longer controls our feeling toward the person.

This is particularly practical in marriage. Husband and wife can remember a failure for years. A large heart does not deny that something happened, but it refuses to preserve the offense as a permanent claim against the other person. The more we are reconciled to God, the more room there is in us for mercy, patience, and receiving others.

The final exercise for the week is therefore relational. When someone disappoints or offends us, we can ask: Is my heart constricted here? Can I bring this feeling to the Lord, receive more of His salvation, forgive, and let the record go? Such enlargement is a visible fruit of reconciliation.

3 · IMPORTANT SENTENCES
How large our heart is depends on how fully we have allowed God to reconcile and save us.
Enlargement does not mean looseness: be strict with yourself, but spacious and receiving toward others.
Forgiving becomes practical when we stop preserving the offense as an inward record against the other person.
